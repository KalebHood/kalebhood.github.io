**Abstract**
Lateral-force atomic force microscopy (LF-AFM) is a key characterization method in friction studies of graphene, a promising
nanomaterial for low-friction coatings[1]. LF-AFM enables nanoscale friction measurements, essential for probing friction prop-
erties of small areas of the coating. Two key factors for graphene’s performance as a friction reducing layer are graphene grain
size[2] and thickness[3]. Prior friction studies of graphene on steel, featuring transferred graphene, found that thicker, large grain
size graphene has the lowest friction. The study presented here uncovers the effects of thickness and grain size for graphene that is
directly grown on 8620 steel by chemical vapor deposition (CVD). This CVD coating is a mixture of multilayer graphene (MLG),
and few-layer graphene (FLG). MLG areas have large grain sizes and a thicker coating, while FLG areas have smaller grains, and a
thinner coating. The CVD coating thickness can be controlled to be dominantly MLG or FLG by changing CVD growth param-
eters[4]. The results of the nanoscale characterization of MLG and FLG areas in this study suggest that CVD grown MLG on
low-alloy steel is superior to FLG. This is because the MLG areas are thicker with larger grain sizes, supporting MLG dominant,
low-friction CVD graphene coatings for steel. Raman and AFM data were obtained with a Horiba HR800 and a Nanosurf
FlexAFM in lateral force mode respectively.
