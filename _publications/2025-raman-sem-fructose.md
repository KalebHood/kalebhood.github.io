**Abstract** 
Fructose, abundant in natural sources such as fruits and vegetables, is one of the three dietary monosaccharides, along with glucose and galactose. Recently, fructose has been investigated [1] as a promising natural organic material for use as a dielectric film in environmentally friendly electronic devices, addressing the global e-waste issue. In this study, we report on the microscopy, spectroscopy, and electrical characterization of fructose films developed as active resistive layers in resistive switching random access memory (ReRAM) devices, a critical component for neuromorphic computing systems.
