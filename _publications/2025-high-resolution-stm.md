**Abstract**
This study describes a method for creating atomically sharp tungsten tips for scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) imaging using a simple microcontroller-based system. Similar to other electrochemical etching setups, this system monitors the resistance between the tip electrode and the counter electrode and cuts off the power when the resistance increases drastically, indicating that the tip has been fully etched. However, the key difference here is that the etching parameters are controlled via variables in the firmware of the microcontroller rather than being set by individual electronic components. This allows for simple modification of performance for various etchant solutions or electrode spacings. Single, atomically sharp tips are required for STM to extract electrons from extremely localized regions of the sample [1]. In order to obtain atomic sharpness during electrochemical etching, it is necessary to stop the current through the electrodes just as the bottom part of the tip falls away, avoiding over-etching and blunting the tip [2].
