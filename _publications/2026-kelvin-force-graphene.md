**Abstract**
Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) is an atomic force microscope (AFM) technique well suited for evaluating graphene as an anticorrosive coating for steel, because of the close relationship between electronic work function (ϕ) and electrochemical corrosion potential (Ecorr) [1]. Graphene’s chemical inertness, impermeability, and electronic properties make it a promising corrosion barrier, with work function influenced by layer number, defect density and graphene - substrate interactions. Here, graphene was grown directly with chemical vapor deposition (CVD) on 1095 steel using acetylene and hydrogen forming gases at 600 °C. The experimental design was based on previous results showing how CVD conditions, alloy composition, and microstructures influence graphene layer number and defect density on steel substrates [2]. Characterization of coatings used Raman spectroscopy, tapping mode AFM/KPFM, and electrochemical testing in simulated seawater.
